Permit structures and routing rules

An oversize or overweight permit is permission for one load on one path. Scanning the routing rules means checking that path against bridges, overhead structures, and the roads that load is allowed to use.

Height meets a bridge or a wire. Width meets a lane, a work zone, or a tight structure. Length meets a turn. Weight meets the bridge and the posted road. Those are different limits. A fee does not settle them.

What a scan is

The scan uses the load's height, width, length, and weight, and the truck and trailer that are actually pulling it. The same numbers have to be the ones on the permit. A route that looks fine on a passenger map can still be the wrong road for that combination.

The estimator prices the permit. It does not clear the bridge. Carrier Vehicle Lookup identifies the carrier and the vehicle. It does not pick the highway.

What else can stop a cleared route

A path that clears the structures can still be illegal after dark. A wide load at a tight structure is also when a pilot car shows up. A route that scans clean can still sit still on a holiday blackout.

This page does not run a scan and does not list state bridge formulas. It is not a permit and not legal advice.
