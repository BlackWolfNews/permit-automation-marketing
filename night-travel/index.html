Night travel for oversize loads

Many states keep oversize loads in daylight. A permit that is valid during the day may not allow the truck to move after dark, or may allow it only with extra lights, signs, or an escort.

Night travel is an operating-time rule. It is not a line on the fee. The same load can be legal at mid-morning and illegal at night in the same state. The cutoff is set by that state and by that permit, not by a single national clock.

What people mix up

Paying for the permit does not buy the night. A route that already cleared bridges and routing rules still has to be legal at the hour you roll. Some night moves also require a pilot car. This page does not decide which ones.

The other clock on the same permit is the holiday blackout. A Tuesday-night rule and a Thanksgiving rule are both about when the load may move. They are not the same restriction.

The estimator prices the permit. It does not tell you the legal hour. Carrier Vehicle Lookup identifies the carrier and the vehicle. It does not set operating times.

This page does not publish a 50-state hours chart. Hours differ by state and by load, and a chart printed here would go stale. Not legal advice.
